## Classification

`DESIGN_CLARIFICATION_REQUIRED`

The frozen baseline fixes the safety outcomes but does not uniquely define the same-scope temporal publication contract needed to obtain them. In particular, it does not state whether `supersedes_ref` logically truncates an open-ended predecessor, whether overlapping intervals are valid, or how multiple applicable revisions are resolved. Choosing any of those meanings in implementation would silently create policy semantics.

This is not an architecture conflict. One narrow Accounting-owned clarification can make publication and resolution deterministic while preserving all frozen requirements.

## Frozen Semantics Already Determined

- `BudgetPolicyRevision` is immutable and revisioned separately from immutable Graph topology.
- A reservation pins the exact `budget_policy_revision_ref` set used for its authorization; later publication must not rewrite that historical pin or re-evaluate an existing `RESERVED` or `DENIED` reservation.
- Later revisions must be able to govern future reservations.
- Every applicable HARD rule on the complete AccountingScope ancestry must be evaluated atomically.
- A child policy cannot erase or widen an ancestor HARD restriction. Policy selection is per scope; ancestry composition is cumulative, not child-over-parent replacement.
- `budget_policy_revision_ref` is identity only. Lexicographic ordering of that ref has no frozen semantic authority.

## Why an Implementation-Local Rule Is Insufficient

The fields admit multiple incompatible but superficially plausible interpretations:

- `effective_from` identifies when a revision may begin to apply, but the baseline does not define tie handling for two revisions at the same scope and instant.
- `effective_until?` permits bounded or open-ended intervals, but the baseline does not define interval endpoint convention or whether a later superseding revision implicitly ends an open interval.
- `supersedes_ref?` is present but its required target, same-scope constraint, chain constraint, and effect on applicability are unspecified.
- `budget_policy_revision_ref` must remain an opaque immutable identity and therefore cannot break temporal ties.

A blanket publication rule rejecting every same-scope overlap would be safe in isolation but would violate the required evolution path already exercised by Task 074: an open-ended v1 followed by a later v2 necessarily overlaps unless either v1 is mutated (forbidden) or supersession is given an implicit logical-truncation meaning (not frozen). Conversely, allowing overlap and selecting by latest `effective_from` still leaves equal-effective conflicts and branching supersession unresolved.

Failing reservation resolution whenever more than one revision is applicable would prevent an unsafe authorization, but it does not supply a usable publication/resolution contract and can strand future reservations after a publication the current API accepts. It is an appropriate temporary guard, not closure of F-002.

## Minimum Required Clarification

The Design Authority should freeze one linear, same-scope revision-chain rule with these decisions:

1. **Interval convention:** state whether applicability is the half-open interval `[effective_from, effective_until)` (matching the reviewed implementation) or another exact convention.
2. **Successor authority:** state whether a valid direct successor's `effective_from` logically truncates its predecessor for current-selection purposes without mutating the predecessor record. This is the narrowest rule compatible with immutable open-ended history and later publication.
3. **Publication invariants:** define whether every non-initial same-scope revision must name exactly the current chain tip in `supersedes_ref`; require the target to exist, belong to the same `accounting_scope_ref`, precede the successor, and not already have another successor; reject self-reference, cycles, missing/cross-scope targets, branches, and equal `effective_from` within a scope.
4. **Explicit-end consistency:** define the required relationship between a predecessor's non-null `effective_until` and its successor's `effective_from` (for example equality, or whether gaps are allowed). Reject any pair that violates the chosen rule.
5. **Scheduled publication:** define whether more than one future successor may be pre-published and, if so, require a single unambiguous chain with strictly increasing `effective_from` values.
6. **Current selection:** at reservation time `t`, select the unique revision on that scope's valid chain whose effective interval contains `t`, with any logical successor boundary applied. Zero applicable revisions means no scope-local policy; more than one applicable revision or an invalid/ambiguous chain must fail closed. No identifier ordering participates.

The smallest compatible clarification is therefore: immutable records form one non-branching chain per scope; time order is strictly increasing; valid direct supersession supplies the effective boundary for an otherwise open predecessor; and current selection is by the unique interval containing the reservation decision time.

## Required Handling of the Listed Cases

- **Same-scope equal-`effective_from` conflicts:** reject at publication; existing ambiguous data must fail closed at resolution. This requires the clarification above to establish uniqueness as contract, not identifier ordering.
- **Overlapping effective intervals:** reject unless the clarified supersession rule defines the successor boundary that removes the logical overlap. Do not mutate historical rows.
- **`supersedes_ref` consistency:** validate as a same-scope, direct, non-branching predecessor link under an Accounting Owner transaction.
- **Deterministic current revision selection:** select the unique chain interval containing the decision time; never sort by `budget_policy_revision_ref`.
- **Immutable history:** append revisions only; never update/delete a prior policy or rewrite `policy_revision_refs` on a reservation.
- **Later policy publication:** a valid later successor governs only reservations decided in its interval; prior reservation pins and states remain unchanged.
- **Ancestor HARD preservation:** resolve at most one current revision independently for every scope in the pinned ancestry, then cumulatively evaluate every HARD rule from every resolved ancestor. A child revision has no operation that removes or weakens an ancestor's rule.

## Safe Interim Behavior Pending Clarification

Publication should not accept new ambiguous same-scope equal-effective, overlapping, cross-scope-superseding, or branching records. Reservation resolution should fail closed if the stored rows do not yield exactly one unambiguous applicable revision for a scope. This interim containment is not the final semantic fix because the valid successor/interval rules remain a Design Authority decision.
